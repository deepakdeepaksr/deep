## 1. What the candidate is, in datasheet terms

The Robu page and the supplied `buck` file state: input 4–38 V, output 1.25–36 V adjustable,
output 0–5 A (recommended below 4.5 A), over-temperature protection, "better than 2596, easy to
4 A 50 W, up to 75 W adding the heat sink". **Those are vendor numbers, not silicon numbers.**
The XL4015E1 data sheet says something narrower:

| Parameter | XL4015E1 data sheet (XLSEMI Rev 2.0) | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Recommended operating input | **8 V to 36 V** | p.1 features, p.5 DC parameters |
| Absolute maximum input | **−0.3 V to 40 V** | p.4 Absolute Maximum Ratings |
| Output adjustable | **1.25 V to 32 V** (vendor page says 36 V) | p.1 |
| Feedback voltage | 1.225 / 1.25 / 1.275 V (**±2 %**) | p.5 |
| Switch current limit | **7 A typical** | p.5 |
| Power PMOS Rds(on) | 60 typical / 80 max mΩ at 5 A | p.5 |
| Efficiency | **87 % typ at 12 V→5 V, 5 A**; 93 % typ at 24 V→12 V, 4 A | p.5 |
| Oscillator | 180 kHz (144–216) | p.5 |
| Quiescent supply current | 2.1 typ / 5 max mA | p.5 |
| Thermal resistance RθJA | **30 °C/W**, TO263-5L, no heatsink, free air | p.4 |
| Operating junction | −40 to +125 °C; thermal shutdown built in, **threshold not specified** | p.4, p.1 |
| Dropout / max duty | 0.3 V min / 100 % max | p.1 |

Two internal inconsistencies are in the vendor's own text and matter for the compliance claims:

1. **4 V input is impossible for a 5.15 V output.** This is a buck: at best Vout = Vin − 0.3 V
   at 100 % duty, so 4 V in gives 3.7 V out, and the silicon itself is not specified below 8 V.
2. **The module's 38 V claim is above the IC's recommended ceiling (36 V).** The only number at
   or above 38 V is the absolute maximum, which the data sheet notes is "a stress rating only and
   functional operation … above those indicated in the operation is not implied".

The module-level data sheet for the same part family (Handson PSU1031, the 51 × 26.3 × 14 mm
CC/CV board) states input **8–36 Vdc**, output 1.25–32 V, voltage regulation **±2.5 %**, load
regulation ±0.5 %, output ripple **50 mV max**, and module operating temperature −40 to **+85 °C**.

## 2. Electrical compliance — condition 1 and B1/B2/B8

### 2.1 The 40 V input clause (condition 1, first sentence)

> "Input rated 40 V or more and covering the charge controller's disconnect voltage (B8)"

| Candidate | Recommended operating input | Absolute max | Meets "40 V or more"? |
|---|---|---|---|
| XL4015 (this module) | 8–36 V | 40 V | **No** — 36 V, and the abs max equals the minimum acceptable rating |
| XL4016 (8–12 A sibling, audited for completeness) | 8–36 V | 40 V | **No** — identical ceiling |
| LM2596 (TI, if that die is in the "HVS" module) | 4.5–40 V | 45 V | **Just** — 40 V exactly |
| LM2596HV (UMW/UTD data sheet) | 4.75–60 V | 63 V | **Yes** |

The 22–25 V disconnect event of B8 is inside all four. The clause that separates them is the
surge part, because condition 1 and B1 require the *clamp* to sit under the converter's maximum
input, not under its absolute maximum.

### 2.2 The 40 V TVS clamp clause (B1)

B1's own worked example is the test. Verbatim from 004 §B1: "A unidirectional TVS with standoff
above the highest input it must survive and clamp below the buck's maximum input … an SMBJ24A or
SMCJ24A stands off 24 V and clamps at about 39 V at its rated current, so pair it with a buck
rated 60 V." Measured against the Taiwan Semi SMBJ table:

| Part | Standoff Vrwm | Breakdown min | Clamp Vc at Ipp | Conducts at the 22–25 V disconnect? | Clamp inside XL4015's 36 V? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SMBJ18A | 18 V | 20.0 V | 29.2 V | yes, hard | yes |
| SMBJ20A | 20 V | 22.2 V | 32.4 V | yes | yes |
| SMBJ22A | 22 V | 24.4 V | 35.5 V | yes (past 24.4 V) | just |
| **SMBJ24A** | **24 V** | **26.7 V** | **38.9 V @ 16 A** | **no — 25 V is below 26.7 V** | **No: 38.9 V > 36 V** |
| SMBJ26A | 26 V | 28.9 V | 42.1 V | no | No |
| SMBJ28A | 28 V | 31.1 V | 45.4 V | no | No |

**There is no TVS that satisfies both halves of B1 for a 36 V-rated converter.** A 600 W/1500 W
unilateral TVS clamps at 1.52–1.62 × its standoff, so holding it off at 25 V forces the clamp to
about 39–42 V. The XL4015's window closes at 36 V operating, 40 V absolute. Only a 22 V-part
clamps low enough, and it breaks down at 24.4 V — inside the disconnect window, which is exactly
the failure B1's acceptance text forbids ("no TVS current at the highest charge voltage" and the
field impact it describes: "Parts at the low end of the spread conduct continuously, overheat and
usually fail short, which then blows F1").

The LM2596HVS passes this test with the CTO's own example part (SMBJ24A clamps 38.9 V against a
40 V operating / 45 V absolute ceiling), thin but inside; an LM2596HV die passes it with 21 V to
spare. **The XL4015 fails it structurally, not by a small margin.**

### 2.3 Set point 5.15 V ±1 % (condition 1) and B2's 5 % window

±1 % of 5.15 V is ±52 mV. The part's own numbers: XL4015 feedback 1.25 V ±2 % (initial), module
voltage regulation ±2.5 % and load regulation ±0.5 % (Handson). Worst-case stack: 2 + 2.5 + 0.5 =
**±5 % open loop**, before pot drift, contact resistance and temperature. LM2596 is worse on the
reference (1.23 V, ±3 % at 25 °C, ±4 % over junction temperature).

Conclusion: **neither module meets the ±1 % clause as a specification.** Both can only be *made*
to meet it by bench trim against a 4½-digit meter and locking the pot, which converts a rated
accuracy into a set-up-and-forget value with no monitored correction. That is a deviation to be
signed, not a number to be claimed. B2's own acceptance ("5 V within 5 percent from 9 V to the
highest input in B8, at full load") is reachable by both: 9 V is inside the XL4015's 8 V floor and
the LM2596's 4.5 V floor; 5 % of 5.15 V is ±258 mV.

The LED voltmeter on this variant is **not evidence of the set point**: parts of this class are
0.1 V resolution and roughly ±1 % + 1 digit, i.e. about ±150 mV at 5.15 V — three times the
tolerance the condition asks for.

### 2.4 Droop 50 mV for the 0.15 → 2.6 A step (condition 1)

The module's steady-state output ripple is already specified at 50 mV max. Its output capacitor is
a general-purpose electrolytic (330–470 µF/25 V in the reference designs), whose ESR at 180 kHz is
commonly 0.1–0.3 Ω: a 2.45 A step gives an immediate 245–735 mV excursion at the terminals before
the loop responds, and the loop is internally compensated at 180 kHz. **This clause is at high
risk on both candidates and can only be closed by the measurement condition 6 asks for.** It is
not a differentiator between them, and it is not the reason for this audit's verdict.

### 2.5 No-load current, B5

The XL4015 die draws 2.1–5 mA. The 3-digit LED voltmeter on the audited variant draws **5–15 mA
typical, under 20 mA maximum** according to its own data sheet, permanently. The LM2596 die draws
5–10 mA and its module has a single power LED. Taking 15 mA at the 12.8 V battery is 0.19 W and
about 0.36 Ah per day, which is a third of the whole node's sleep budget in the architecture the
review assumes. **The meter variant makes B5 worse for no function the installer can read through
a closed IP65 lid.**

## 3. Output current and thermal — B2's "+70 °C inside the closed box"

Condition 1: "3 A continuous at +70 °C inside the closed box." The vendor's 4 A / 50 W figure is
obtained at 25 °C free air with a heatsink; the `buck` text itself says "up to 75W **adding** the
heat sink", so the heatsink is not part of the item quoted.

Loss model at Vout = 5.15 V, Iout = 3 A, Vin = 15 V (the top of the normal 10–15 V range),
duty ≈ 0.34. Assumptions are declared, not measured:

| Element | Basis | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| XL4015 switch conduction | I²·Rds(on)·D, 60 mΩ typ (p.5) | 0.19 W |
| XL4015 switching | 180 kHz, 15 V / 3 A, 40 ns total transition (assumed) | 0.16 W |
| XL4015 quiescent + VC | Vin × 3 mA | 0.05 W |
| **XL4015 die total** | | **≈ 0.4 W → ΔTj ≈ 12 °C at RθJA 30 °C/W** |
| Catch diode (MBRD1045-class Schottky) | 0.55 V × 3 A × (1 − D) | 1.1 W |
| Inductor (47 µH/5 A, DCR 30–50 mΩ assumed) | I²·R + core | 0.3–0.5 W |
| Voltmeter | 5.15 V × 15 mA | 0.08 W |
| **Module total** | cross-checks with the 87 % typ efficiency at 5 A | **≈ 2.5–3 W** |

**Junction:** Tj ≈ 70 + 12 = **82 °C** at 3 A. The silicon has real margin; this is where the
XL4015 genuinely beats the incumbent.

The same model for the LM2596 uses its specified saturation voltage instead of Rds(on):
VSAT = 1.16 V typ at 3 A (TI p.5) → die loss = 1.16 × 3 × 0.34 = **1.2 W**, and RθJA is 50 °C/W for
TO-263 with 0.5 in² of copper (TI p.4). ΔTj ≈ 60 °C → **Tj ≈ 130 °C at 3 A in +70 °C air**, above
the 125 °C operating limit. That is the arithmetic behind the register's "≈2 A continuous on its
heatsink", and it is the incumbent's real weakness.

**What the XL4015 module still fails, at +70 °C, is everything around the die:**

| Part on the module | Its rating | +70 °C box | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3-digit LED voltmeter | −10 to **+65 °C** operating | 70 °C air | **Out of spec** |
| Input/output electrolytics | module doc says −40 to +85 °C; general-purpose stock is 85 °C | 70 °C air + 10–25 °C local rise from the 1.1 W diode | At or past rating; B3 asks for **105 °C long-life throughout** |
| PCB laminate of the module | uncontrolled-glass type, unknown | 70 °C | Unknown, not claimed |

Enclosure budget, as a check on the +70 °C assumption: the box (200 × 155 × 80 mm) has 0.119 m² of
surface; at h ≈ 10 W/m²K for natural convection plus radiation, **every watt inside raises the air
about 0.8 K**, so the module's own 2.5–3 W is worth **roughly 25 K**. Whether that lands on the
+70 °C figure or pushes past it is exactly what open register question B12 ("hottest air in the
box") was asked to bound.

## 4. Protection coordination — a new risk the XL4015 introduces

F1 is a 4 A-hold device on glass-fuse clips (finding B3, unfixed), needing roughly 8 A to trip.
The converters' own limits: LM2596 3.6 min / 4.5 typ / 6.9 max A peak switch current; XL4015
**7 A typ**, folding the oscillator to 48 kHz on a short. So with the XL4015 the fault current that
the converter will hold sits between F1's hold and trip, and the copper in between is the 1.5 mm
input track of R5. Interpolating the review's own Table 6 (1.0 mm at 4 A = +32 K, 3 mm at 4 A =
+5 K) puts 1.5 mm at about +20 K at 4 A, and heating scales near I², so a sustained **7 A is about
+60 K on that track — in a +70 °C box.** The LM2596's lower limit is one of the few things still
protecting that copper.

This is an argument for condition 2 (the board's own over-voltage and reverse cut-off with a
current limit near 3.5 A), not against the XL4015 alone: whichever converter is named, the 5 A
class makes the missing U10 circuit more dangerous, because the module's output is adjustable to
32–36 V and the board has nothing between J18 and its 5 V loads.

## 5. Footprint and mechanical

Verified on `pcb\MoriShield_Carrier.kicad_pcb` (evidence `pcb\_evidence\power_chain_pads.json`):

| Item | State today |
|---|---|
| U10 | value `eFuse`, `Package_TO_SOT_SMD:SOT-23-6` at (141, 100.8). **All six pads carry local "unconnected" names; no pad has a circuit net.** The register's "five pads unconnected" understates it by one |
| Buck footprint or socket | **None.** J9 (Rev A buck socket), U1, D1 and C6 are gone from both the board and the schematic |
| Power path as built | J18 pad 1 `+5V_IN` → F1 → `+5V_F` → Q9 (AO3401A: 1 `GND`, 2 `+5V_F`, 3 `+5V`) → `+5V` → J5 pad 1; D2 (SMCJ5.0A) and ESD16 (SMBJ5.0A) on `+5V`; C13 10 µF/50 V on `+5V`; D7 (SS56) to `/+5V_ESP` |

**A 61 × 38 × 13 mm through-hole module (vendor-stated for the meter variant; 51 × 26.3 × 14 mm
for the CC/CV variant) has no place on the carrier**, and U10 is not a buck footprint: it is the
SOT-23-6 placeholder for condition 2's protection controller. Adopting any off-board module —
XL4015 or LM2596HVS — therefore requires **no new footprint and no layout change**. Bringing a
converter back on-board would be a different project: it was withdrawn with the CTO's written
approval (register answer I6, "the buck ran hot, no 12 V is needed anywhere, keeping the board at
5 V is safer"), and it needs a module outline, a new power footprint, 3 mm pour and bulk capacitance
that Rev C's area does not hold.

Mounting and clearance are the real mechanical questions, and they are the same for both modules.
The arithmetic on the agreed box (200 × 155 × 80 mm, acrylic base plate — register answers D1, D8)
against the 178.15 × 118.15 mm carrier:

| Where the module could go | Space | 61 × 38 × 13 mm module | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Floor beside the carrier, board pushed to one edge | 155 × 36.85 mm strip | 38 mm wide against 36.85 mm | **1.15 mm short**; the other strip (21.85 × 155 mm) is far too narrow |
| Under the carrier | M3 × 8 mm standoffs (register answer D3 default) give 8 mm | 13–14 mm tall | **Does not fit** |
| On the lid, facing down | 200 × 155 available | fits | Needs the 3 W diode and inductor pointing at the sealed lid, which is where the box's hottest air is |
| On a bracket or the plate at the cable end | fits | fits | **The workable option**, and it is what the incumbent was recorded against ("mounted on the acrylic plate with 5 mm air", archived work register W21) |

The meter variant adds two further problems wherever it goes: the display is unreadable through a
closed IP65 lid, and it is the part with the lowest temperature rating on the whole product.

## 7. Recommendation and next steps

**No-Go for the XL4015-with-LED-voltmeter module as condition 1's named converter.** It buys real
current and die-thermal headroom, and it spends the one clause the incumbent passes and the CTO's
B1 test is built around: a converter whose 36 V ceiling is below the clamp of any TVS that can sit
behind a 24–25 V disconnect. The meter variant additionally puts a +65 °C part and a permanent
10–20 mA load inside a box speced to +70 °C, and raises the fault current the board's un-upgraded
input copper and F1 have to carry.

If the CTO weights 3 A hot above the input-rating clause, the only defensible version of that
trade is the **plain XL4015 or XL4016 module without the voltmeter**, and it needs all four of
these signed, not assumed: (1) a written deviation from "input rated 40 V or more" in condition 1;
(2) a re-coordinated surge plan that says what limits a 38.9–42 V clamp into a 40 V absolute-max
input — a series element or a fusible resistor in the lead, since the TVS alone cannot do it;
(3) a 105 °C electrolytic and display-free build, or a measured box temperature below +65 °C;
(4) condition 2 protection designed on the carrier before any 32–36 V-capable module is wired to
J18.

**Preferred path, and the one to ask Serigreen to fund: name the converter by condition 1's
numbers rather than by what a ₹300 shelf part offers.** Condition 1 asks for ≥40 V, B1's own text
asks for "a buck rated 60 V", and 004's register answer A6 already makes Serigreen the supplier of
every component. The ₹300 ceiling was our own constraint (B13a), not theirs.

Next steps, in order, none of which is a schematic or layout change today:

## 8. What this audit does not claim

No item is marked Done, and none of B1, B2, B8, R4, B10 or condition 1 moves. The schematic and
the board were not touched: the audit's output is a candidate rejection and a question back to
Serigreen. The XL4015 module in hand has not been measured — no dimensions, capacitor grades, pot
type, short-circuit current or droop figure in this file is a measurement; each is labelled by its
source. The efficiency figures are the data sheet's typical values at 5 A and 12 V, not at our
3 A and 15 V, which is why §3 leads with the loss model.
