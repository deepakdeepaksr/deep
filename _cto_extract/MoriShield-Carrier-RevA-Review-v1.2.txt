1. Summary
Package v3 of 12 October changes the layout only. J1 and J4 have moved, the tracks to them are re-routed, both
ground pours are refilled and the J1 label now sits beside its socket. The refill matters: in the 6 October files the
ground pours overlapped +12V, +5V and FSR_ADC copper, so boards made from those files would have had all three
shorted to ground; v3 has no such overlap. The schematic, BOM, project file and schematic PDF are byte-for-byte
identical to the 6 October package, so v3 closes none of the 46 requests below (E5 is partly closed). This review had
not reached you when v3 was prepared, so that is expected. The socket moves themselves need evidence before
anything else moves: the new row spacings (26.00 mm for the ESP32 board, 33.56 mm for the modem) are not
whole multiples of the 2.54 mm header grid, the rows are offset by 0.30 mm and 0.50 mm, and the README still asks
for both to be checked against the physical boards. The 26.00 mm spacing also no longer fits the Espressif DevKitC-1
that A6 proposes.
Electrically, v3 hangs together well. The netlist re-derived from the schematic matches the routed PCB pad for pad, every net is
joined in copper, the pours keep their clearances, the Gerbers match the PCB object for object, and the layout passes DRC,
although DRC cannot see module bodies or the enclosure (A4). It is not ready to fabricate for the field. Judged as the
field-ready MoriShield node, the board lacks the wind (RS485), local logging, FDC1004, event-button and status channels the
node design requires. The two largest plug-in boards are not confirmed to match their sockets, and the ESP32 module in the
order is rated only to +65 °C. The input stage suits a mains adapter rather than the solar kit, there is no surge protection,
earthing or hardware watchdog, and the board does not fit the enclosure in the order.
Section 8 sets out 46 change requests, each with its status in v3. Please start with A1, A2 and A6, which settle the two large
modules; the pin map and most other items depend on them. Please hold further layout changes until A1, A2, A4 and A6 are
answered, because they decide where the sockets go, the pin map and the power design.

5. Direction for Rev B
Serigreen has decided the following for the pilot build.
1. Plug-in modules
Accepted for the pilot build, on four conditions: modules held down mechanically (standoffs, clamps or ties); orientation
keyed or clearly marked; carrier and modules conformal-coated with contacts masked (H4); desiccant and a breather
vent in the box. A custom PCB remains the production path.
2. 4G per unit
Optional. One carrier builds two variants: a LoRa node, and a LoRa plus 4G uplink node. 4G is not required on every unit.
3. Power
An off-the-shelf solar charge controller with a 12.8 V LiFePO4 battery. The carrier input runs at 10 to 15 V in normal use.
Input parts are rated 40 V or more, because a battery or protection-circuit disconnect can put panel voltage (about 22 V
open-circuit) on the load terminals of some controllers (B8). The step-down keeps working down to about 9 V.
4. Two layers
Accepted, provided the FDC1004 electronics stay on their own breakout at the electrode, not on the carrier.
5. Soil
The analog soil probe is accepted for now. The RS485 7-in-1 plugs into the wind bus later.

8.1 Module fit and pin map
A1 · ESP32-S3 board identity and socket pin order
P0
Status in v3
Open, changed in v3: v3 moved J1 by 3.14 mm across and 0.30 mm along the row; the README says this still needs
checking against the physical module.
Where
J1, J2: two 1 x 22 sockets. In v3, pin 1 at (209.50, 154.14) and (183.50, 153.84): rows 26.00 mm apart, with J1 0.30 mm lower
than J2. In the 6 Oct package the rows were 22.86 mm apart and aligned.
Finding
The sockets follow Espressif's ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1 pin order exactly; v3 moved J1 without changing the pin order. The board
in Robu order SO283553 is an EdgeHax ESP32-S3 N16R8 with two USB-C ports and its own microSD slot, for which we found
no published pinout. README item 3 gives I2C-0 on GPIO21/22, I2C-1 on GPIO47/48 and EN on GPIO18; the schematic uses
IO8/IO9 for I2C-0, IO4/IO5 for I2C-1 and IO18 for LoRa RX. The ESP32-S3 has no GPIO22. The v3 README still asks for the
26.0 mm gap to be verified against the physical module. 26.00 mm is not a whole multiple of the 2.54 mm grid that board
headers are normally placed on, and it no longer fits Espressif's own DevKitC-1 (22.86 mm). If the EdgeHax board's headers
sit on that grid, the nearest spacing is 25.40 mm, 0.60 mm from v3's, enough to force the pins sideways in the sockets.
Field impact
A pin-order or spacing mismatch makes every unit dead on arrival. A microSD slot wired to IO10 to IO14 would collide with
the modem control and UART lines.
Change required
Fix the exact board, taking A6 into account, and send a measured drawing of its headers (row spacing, row offset, pin 1
positions) or a test-fit before moving the sockets again. Match socket pin order, row spacing and row alignment to it, and
keep carrier signals off the pins used by its microSD slot, RGB LED and USB-serial chip.
Acceptance
Photos of both sides of the board; the measured header drawing; a pin table signed by hardware and firmware; one board
test-fitted in sockets on a bare PCB or a 1:1 paper template.
A2 · LTE modem board identity, supply and interface
P0
Status in v3
Open, changed in v3: v3 moved J4 2.00 mm closer to J3 and 0.50 mm along the row; the supply question is untouched.
Where
J3, J4: two 1 x 12 sockets, in v3 at x 235.50 and 269.06 (33.56 mm apart; 35.56 mm on 6 Oct), with J4 pin 1 0.50 mm lower
than J3 (y 99.00 against 98.50). J5: modem 5 V feed at (286.5, 136.5). J3 pin 3 (VDD_IN_UART) is on the 3.3 V sensor rail.
Finding
J5 feeds the modem 5 V. The A7672S board in the order is listed at 3.4 to 4.2 V, and its photo shows a battery connector;
SIMCom specifies 3.4 to 4.2 V for the module, with peaks of about 2 A, and a 1.8 V UART. The README, unchanged on this
point in v3, calls 5 V "within module tolerance" and describes the header as 9 + 2 pins, while the PCB has two 12-pin rows,
moved in v3 to 33.56 mm apart and offset by 0.50 mm. 33.56 mm is not a whole multiple of 2.54 mm; the nearest grid
spacing, 33.02 mm, is 0.54 mm away. PWR_EN, PWRKEY and RESET go to pins of that assumed header, so whether
firmware can power-cycle or reset the modem depends on the real board. The drawing-layer note still reads "A7672S
MODEM VERIFY 35.56mm".
Field impact
5 V on a 4.2 V supply input destroys the modem at first power-up. A wrong header map leaves the uplink dead.
Change required
Identify the board (link, datasheet, photos). If its input is the module supply, add a dedicated 3.8 V rail rated for 2 A bursts,
switched by firmware so the modem can be hard power-cycled, with bulk capacitance at the connector (see B4). If a board
with its own wide-input regulator and level shifter is chosen instead, feed it from the 12 V input. Redraw J3 and J4 to a
measured drawing of the header, and send that drawing before moving them again. Confirm the UART level shifter and
what J3 pin 3 expects. Drive PWRKEY and RESET open-drain with defined default states (SIMCom: never hold both low
together; no more than 100 nF on either line). Confirm the module variant covers the Indian LTE bands.
Acceptance
Datasheet extract and photos. On a bench unit, the modem supply measured during a transmit burst stays inside its range,
and the modem registers on the network.

A6 · ESP32 module temperature rating
P0
Status in v3
Open: v3's 26.00 mm row spacing no longer fits the Espressif DevKitC-1-N8R2 proposed here.
Where
The ESP32-S3 board on J1, J2. The board in the order carries an ESP32-S3-WROOM-1-N16R8 module.
Finding
Espressif rates WROOM-1 modules with octal PSRAM (R8, such as N16R8) for -40 to +65 °C ambient; quad-PSRAM (R2) and
no-PSRAM variants are rated -40 to +85 °C. With PSRAM ECC enabled, R8 modules may run to +85 °C at the cost of 1/16 of
the PSRAM. The node design assumes +70 °C inside a sun-exposed box and asks for parts rated to +85 °C.
Field impact
The processor module would run above its rating on hot afternoons, which is when spraying, drift and alerts matter most.
Change required
Use a board with an N8R2, N8 or N16 module. Espressif's ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1-N8R2 has the DevKitC-1 pin order and frees
GPIO35 to 37 for A5, but its rows are 22.86 mm apart: it fits the 6 Oct sockets, not v3's 26.00 mm. Some boards sold under
the DevKitC-1 name are 25.4 mm between rows, so measure the one bought. Decide A1 and A6 together. If N16R8 boards
must be used, enable PSRAM ECC in firmware and record the decision.
Acceptance
Photo of the module marking; datasheet rating of +85 °C or higher, or ECC enabled and confirmed in the firmware build.
8.2 Power
B1 · Input TVS
P1
Status in v3
Open
Where
D2 at (144.5, 131.5), on +12V after F1 and D1.
Finding
The SMBJ12CA is bidirectional, with a 12 V standoff and a breakdown of 13.3 to 14.7 V at 1 mA. Behind D1 it sees about
14.1 to 14.3 V while a LiFePO4 or lead-acid battery charges at 14.4 to 14.6 V, which is inside its breakdown spread.
Field impact
Parts at the low end of the spread conduct continuously, overheat and usually fail short, which then blows F1. The unit stays
dead until someone visits.
Change required
A unidirectional TVS with standoff above the highest input it must survive and clamp below the buck's maximum input,
sized together with B2 and the battery-disconnect case in B8. For example, an SMBJ24A or SMCJ24A stands off 24 V and
clamps at about 39 V at its rated current, so pair it with a buck rated 60 V. Use the 1500 W (SMC) size at the cable entry,
with H1.
Acceptance
Clamp simulation (Gate Pack simulation 3) with documented values; bench run at the highest charge voltage with no
measurable TVS current.
B2 · Buck converter input range
P1
Status in v3
Open
Where
J9: plug-in 12 to 24 V to 5 V, 3 A module.
Finding
The module is listed for 12 to 24 V. After D1's drop it runs below that range once J18 falls under about 12.4 V, which a 12.8
V LiFePO4 reaches late in its discharge (it rests near 13 V and falls towards 10 V). The clamp of any TVS that tolerates charge
voltage is above 24 V.
Field impact
Behaviour below 12 V is unknown (dropout, oscillation or brownout resets at night), and there is no margin above 24 V.
Change required
A buck, module or on-board, rated 60 V input (40 V at the very least, see B1), working from about 6 V, delivering 5 V at 3 A

ideal-diode controller in place of D1 to save its 0.3 to 0.5 V drop.
Acceptance
5 V within 5 percent from 9 V to the highest input in B8, at full load including a modem burst (simulation, then bench);
temperature rise measured inside the closed box.

P1
Status in v3
Open
Where

P1

C6 · I2C bus structure
P1
Status in v3
Open
Where
I2C-0 (IO8, IO9; R1, R2 at 1k8) and I2C-1 (IO4, IO5; R3, R4 at 4k7).
Finding
I2C-0 carries the SGP41, SHT45, BME688, SPS30, RTC and expansion port, mostly over cables. Each 1k8 pull-up alone draws
about 1.6 mA when a device pulls its line to 0.4 V; with the RTC board's 4.7 k and three typical 10 k breakout pull-ups, the

Sensors that are switched off can be back-powered through pull-ups tied to an always-on rail.
Field impact
One corroded connector or latched sensor takes down every channel and the timestamp.
Change required
I2C-1 for on-board modules only: RTC 0x68, its EEPROM 0x57, the MPU-6050 with AD0 tied high (0x69) and the GPIO

H2 · Watchdog and recovery
P1
Status in v3
Open
Where
J1 pin 3 (ESP32 reset, unconnected; at (209.50, 149.06) in v3); the 5 V rail to the ESP32 board and the E22 (unswitched).
Finding
The node design requires a hardware watchdog, brownout detection and a safe restart. On Rev A the ESP32 reset pin is
unconnected, and the ESP32 board and E22 sit on an unswitched 5 V rail. Only the modem can be power-cycled, and only if
its board's PWR_EN works as assumed.
Field impact
A hung ESP32 or E22 needs a site visit.
Change required
An external supervisor or watchdog that resets the ESP32 (J1 pin 3) or power-cycles the 5 V loads if firmware stops servicing

either). In both cases, a switch on the E22 supply, since an ESP32 restart does not reset the E22, and defined behaviour for
the slow supply ramps a solar kit gives at dawn.
Acceptance
A firmware hang and a slow supply ramp (0 to 15 V over several minutes) both end in a clean restart without intervention.

Appendix C. Radio rules for the E22 in India
Rule
G.S.R. 853(E), 10 December 2021: Use of Low Power Equipment in the Frequency Band 865 to 868 MHz for Short Range
Devices (Exemption from Licence) Rules, 2021. It replaced the 2005 rules for 865 to 867 MHz.
Category
Tracking, tracing and data acquisition (includes sensors and meter reading).
Limits
500 mW e.r.p.; duty cycle 2.5 percent or less (10 percent or less for network access points); bandwidth 200 kHz or less;
adaptive power control required; standard EN 300 220.
Approval
Equipment must be type approved (Rule 5).
This board
E22 at 22 dBm with the 1.8 dBi antenna in the order gives about 150 mW e.r.p. LoRa at 125 kHz bandwidth is inside the limit.
The factory channel, 868.125 MHz, is outside the band and must be changed (C5).
