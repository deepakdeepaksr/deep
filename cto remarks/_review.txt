1. Verdict
Rev B is a real step forward, and it is not ready to print. The board has been rebuilt around a continuous ground
plane, switched sensor supplies, an I2C switch with one segment per cabled port, protection returned to an earth
terminal, and a watchdog. Of the 59 open items in MS-CAR-REV-A-004, 6 are closed, 24 are partly done, 28 are still
open and 1 no longer applies. This review adds 17 new items, 5 of them blocking. Both variants have a fault in their
production files, and the package does not yet carry the evidence the release gate asks for (section 4).

5. Blocking findings in the production files
5.1 The 6-layer Gerbers short the ESP32 header (R1)
In the 6-layer board J1 sits at x 209.25 mm; in the 4-layer it is at 209.5. The 6-layer file's plane fills, and the Gerbers plotted
from them, still have J1's clearance holes centred on 209.5. Every J1 pad on In1 and In2 now meets the edge of the plane: the
gap is under 0.001 mm, which no process can etch, so in practice pad and plane are one piece of copper; pin 1, the square pad,
meets it along a whole edge. Every J1 pin then joins ground on In1 and 5 V on In2, and pin 22, which is ground, joins the 5 V
plane directly. A board made from these files would short its 5 V supply to ground at the first power-up.
Figure 1. In1 ground plane around J1 pins 1 to 4, read from each variant's Gerbers. Left, 4-layer: a 0.25 mm clearance all round. Right, 6-layer: the
pads have moved 0.25 mm and touch the plane, pin 1 (square) along its whole edge. In2 is the same with the 5 V plane.
The DRC in the package does not show this because a DRC that refills zones checks the refilled copper in memory, not the fills
saved in the file that the Gerbers come from. The order that prevents it is the one in 004 F2: refill, save, run DRC on the saved
file, then plot. The same move narrows the ESP32 rows to 25.75 mm (section 7). The 6-layer is withdrawn (section 9); the
check stays for every Gerber set.

6.3 Why the path is lossy, and what else it lacks
The legend on J18 is the wrong way round (R3). The silkscreen prints "5v +" beside pad 2, which is ground, and "5v -" beside
pad 1, the +5 V input. Anyone who wires to the legend reverses the supply, and nothing on the board stops it, as the next
paragraph explains. The schematic titles this block "POWER INPUT and REVERSE-POLARITY PROTECTION"; as fitted, Q9 gives
no reverse protection.
Q9 is reversed for its job (R4). Q9 (AO3401A, P-channel) has its source on the input side and its drain on the load. That way
round it conducts in normal use, but its body diode points from the loads back to J18, so a reversed supply drives the loads
negative through the diode: there is no reverse protection. Wired to the legend, the supply drives D2 and ESD16 forward, and
the fault current runs through Q9's body diode, rated 2 A, and through F1, which needs about 8 A to trip, so in practice only
the converter's current limit bounds it; meanwhile every 5 V load except the ESP32 board (behind D7) sits near −1 V. Fitted the
other way (drain to J18, source to the load, gate to ground) the same part would block a reversed supply. It also adds up to 60
mΩ, about half the path. Its heating is not the problem: 0.41 W at the full burst current, but bursts are short, and averaged
over a GSM frame it is about 0.1 W.
Q9's footprint has been edited (R5). The team's DRC flags Q9 as the one footprint that does not match its library. Its pads are
turned 90 degrees, so each land runs 0.6 mm along the lead and 1.475 mm across it, and the gate pad (ground) sits 0.425 mm
from the source pad (+5V_F), against 1.3 mm on the library part. A solder bridge there shorts the input to ground behind F1,
and the lands leave almost no fillet on the one part that carries the whole board's current. Restore the library footprint, for Q9
or whatever replaces it.
Nothing stops an over-voltage (R4). With 12 V on J18, D2 (SMCJ5.0A) clamps at roughly 7 to 9 V and conducts heavily until F1
trips or D2 fails, while every 5 V load sees that voltage; the SPS30 is rated to 5.5 V at most. Q9's gate sits on ground, so the
same 12 V puts −12 V on its gate, the AO3401A's absolute maximum; a battery on charge (about 14 V) or the 22 to 25 V of a
battery disconnect (B8) breaks it. A 5 V screw terminal that looks like every other screw terminal in the box will meet 12 V one
day.
The copper is sized below its fuse (R5). The whole board's current leaves Q9 on a 1 mm stub of 0.5 mm track into a single 0.3
mm via to the In2 plane; the input itself is 0.5 mm track, 13.8 mm from J18 to F1 and 6.8 mm from F1 to Q9. A 0.3 mm via is
commonly sized for about 1 A. F1 holds 4 A indefinitely, and a 4 A-hold PTC such as MF-R400 can carry up to its trip current,
about 8 A, before it is sure to trip (a 5 by 20 mm glass fuse carries 1.5 times its rating for an hour). Already at 4 A, IPC-2221
puts a long 0.5 mm track about 101 °C above ambient (Table 6). The copper has to be sized for everything up to the
protection's trip or limit current, and the protection chosen with the copper in mind. F1 also has a footprint problem:
"PTC_4A" sits on clips for a 5 by 20 mm glass fuse, where a radial PTC cannot be fitted.

7.4 Modem control (R7)
/PWR_EN is driven by GPIO7 and by the expander's P13, with R16 (4.7 kΩ) to ground. At power-up both drivers are inputs and
R16 holds PWR_EN low, which is the right safe state if low means off; like PWRKEY's, that polarity belongs in the truth table
A11 asked for. Once firmware sets both as outputs they can fight, and after an ESP32 reset the expander keeps driving
whatever it last drove. Keep one driver, GPIO7. PWRKEY is held by R15 (1 kΩ to ground) and nothing else, and RESET is gone.
Whether a low PWRKEY means "pressed" depends on the modem board's translator. If A11 confirms automatic power-on,
PWR_EN power-cycling is the whole recovery path, and that is fine; the truth table must say so.
7.5 Watchdog (R8)
The supervisor is a good addition, and three things stop it working with this ESP32 board. First, the TPS3823 has a push-pull
output, and it drives EN, which the ESP32 board's reset button and USB auto-reset circuit also pull low: they fight. The TPS3828
is the same part with an open-drain output, and is the one to use. Second, WDI comes from the expander, which is not reset
with the ESP32 and keeps its last level. After a watchdog reset nothing toggles WDI until the new firmware does, and the
TPS3823 times out after 1.6 s typical, 0.9 s at the least. In USB download mode nothing toggles it at all, so the watchdog resets
the ESP32 in the middle of flashing. Third, U4 is flagged out of the placement file and BOM on the board.
Drive WDI from a pin with no pull-up or pull-down at reset, which stays high-impedance through reset and download mode
(the TPS382x treats a floating WDI as "watchdog off"); or keep it on the expander with a jumper in series for flashing and
firmware that kicks within 0.9 s of reset. Clear U4's three flags.
7.6 The cabled I2C bus can lock the board (R17)
Rev B puts the I/O expander (U8) on I2C-0 beside the switch (U7), upstream of the cabled ports, and U7's reset comes only
from a power-on RC (R24, C29). If a sensor or its cable holds SDA low while its channel is selected, all of I2C-0 is held low.
Firmware can then reach neither U7, to deselect the channel, nor U8, which carries the watchdog kick, the sensor-rail enable
and PWR_EN. The watchdog resets the ESP32, but nothing resets U7, so the bus stays stuck until the box is power-cycled.
Move U8 to I2C-1, as 003 planned, and reset U7 with the ESP32, from the supervisor's reset line or a GPIO.
7.7 Module outlines on the wrong sockets (R14)
J7 has the GY-521's eight-pin pinout (INT on pin 1) and J8 the DS3231's six-pin pinout (32K, SQW, SCL, SDA, VCC, GND). The
outlines and labels on F.Fab and Dwgs.User are the other way round: the sockets have swapped places since v3, but the
drawings have not. The larger DS3231 board, about 38 by 22 mm with its coin cell underneath, actually hangs from J8 over J16,

A.2 The DC budget to the modem board
A burst lasts many time constants of the supply path (004 Appendix A), so the minimum at the modem board is in effect a DC
calculation: the converter's output, less its load-step droop, less the burst current times the resistance of each part of the path.
Assumptions:
Copper
1 oz (35 µm) on all layers, the job file's value; resistivity 1.72 × 10-8 Ωm at 20 °C, corrected by 0.393 percent per °C to
25 °C and to +70 °C (about 17 percent more hot). Series track lengths between pad edges in the 4-layer file, on the
pads as placed (Q9's rotated lands, F1's rounded corners).
Contacts and lead
Screw terminal contacts 3 mΩ each, a typical value (the terminal maker's specification was not available). Lead from
J5 to the modem board 30 mΩ there and back with its contacts, the allocation of condition 5. 004 assumed 45 mΩ for
v3's cable, which would make every burst figure here about 27 mV lower.
F1
20 to 50 mΩ (4 A PTC or 5 by 20 mm glass fuse with its clips): 30 mΩ at 25 °C, 50 mΩ hot.
Q9
AO3401A, 60 mΩ maximum at 4.5 V gate drive and 25 °C (47 mΩ typical; AOS datasheet); 1.4 times that hot. The
BOM names no maker, and second-source parts can be higher.
Planes and via
Q9's 0.3 mm via and output stub 1.5 mΩ; the 5 V and ground planes from Q9 to J5 and back 4 mΩ.
Loads
2.6 A through J18 in the burst, 1.8 A of it to the modem board (Table 15); 0.15 A total and 0.02 A to the modem at
light load, where the conditions column takes the board cold, at 40 mΩ.
Converter and leads
Set point 5.15 V within 1 percent; 50 mV droop at its terminals for the 0.15 to 2.6 A burst step; 15 mΩ from the
converter to J18 there and back.
